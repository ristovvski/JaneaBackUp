## 1. How to read this

1. **A contradiction here is not an accusation.** Rules in this system changed underneath the people using them; two people can both be sincere, both be quoting accurately, and still hold different versions of the same rule. Nothing below assigns fault.
2. **Nothing is merged.** Both sides of every pair survive verbatim, with **which one is later** marked explicitly. Where the corpus does not settle a pair, it is left unsettled — "unresolved" is a finding, not a gap in the work.
3. **Scope limits are load-bearing.** "Not found in the corpus" means not found in five Slack sources (#gtm-engineering, #infra-issues, #lead-generation-team, DM-Melisa, DM-Sergey) and 21 recorded meetings. It does not cover unrecorded calls, huddles, other channels, or documents. Records **R0956–R0958 are OUT-OF-CORPUS** — owner corrections supplied by Nik in the terminal on 2026-08-19, never to be cited as in-corpus evidence.

## 6. Rules stated aloud in meetings but never written anywhere in Slack

*"Everyone assumes, nobody wrote it down."* Each entry below was checked against all 415 Slack records: the rule's own vocabulary returns **no** in-channel statement of the rule. And we also need to notify everyone that automation is going to be paused, that incoming replies will not be forwarded, that notifications are not going to be sent to Slack during that time."* This is the clearest change-management rule in the corpus and it exists **only** in that recording. It has three clauses: **(a)** no Friday deploys, **(b)** big changes at the beginning of the week, **(c)** notify everyone in advance of the pause and its consequences.
